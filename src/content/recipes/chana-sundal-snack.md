steps:
  - Soak the kabuli chana in three times its volume of water for 8 hours or overnight; they should double in size.
  - Drain, put the chana in the pressure cooker with fresh water to cover by two fingers and a pinch of salt. Cook on high to the first whistle, then on low for 4 to 5 whistles. Let the pressure release by itself; a chana should squash easily but not fall apart.
  - Drain the cooked chana in a strainer and let it sit for a few minutes so the surface dries; wet chana makes a soggy sundal.
  - This recipe is oil-free by design. Heat a non-stick kadhai on medium and dry-toast a pinch of cumin, a broken dry red chilli, a few curry leaves and a pinch of hing for 20 to 30 seconds until fragrant, keeping them moving. If you prefer a classic mustard-seed tempering, use half a teaspoon of oil from your own allowance and let the mustard pop first.
  - Add the drained chana, salt, a pinch of turmeric and a splash of water. Toss on medium for 3 to 4 minutes until the chana is coated and dry again.
  - Switch off, add the grated coconut and most of the chopped coriander and toss for 30 seconds in the residual heat so the coconut does not brown.
  - Serve warm or at room temperature with a katori of thick curd per person, the rest of the coriander scattered over the curd.
notes: Sundal keeps 2 days in the fridge and is best eaten at room temperature, which makes it a good tiffin; add the coconut fresh if you are packing it for the next day. Cooked chana can be frozen in portions for later batches.
