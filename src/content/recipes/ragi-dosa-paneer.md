swaps:
  - for: Urad dal batter
    use: 2 tbsp thick curd stirred into the ragi flour with water
    why: Instant version; no soaking or grinding, rest the batter 20 minutes and it still spreads well
  - for: Fresh coconut
    use: Desiccated coconut, 1½ tbsp
    why: Keeps in the cupboard
  - for: Homemade podi spice
    use: 1 tsp of any milagai podi you already have
    why: Skips toasting dal and chillies
steps:
  - Soak all the urad dal in plenty of water for at least 4 hours, or overnight. Drain and grind it in the mixie with a little fresh water, stopping to scrape down, until it is smooth and fluffy.
  - Tip the urad paste into a bowl and whisk in all the ragi flour and the ground flaxseed with enough water to make a batter that drops off the ladle in a thick ribbon. Add salt. For a sour, spongy dosa leave it covered in a warm spot overnight; if you are short on time, rest it 30 minutes and it will still make a good crisp dosa.
  - For the podi filling, dry roast a spoon of chana dal and a dry red chilli in the kadhai on low until the dal turns golden and smells toasty, about 3 minutes. Cool, then grind coarsely with the grated coconut, a pinch of hing and salt.
  - In the same kadhai, warm a few drops of the oil, add the crumbled paneer, the podi mix and the remaining dry masalas (turmeric, a little red chilli). Toss on medium for 2 to 3 minutes until the paneer is coated and warm. Switch off and fold in half the coriander.
  - Heat the tawa on high until a sprinkle of water dances and disappears. Drop the heat to medium, rub a few drops of oil over the surface with half an onion or a folded cloth.
  - Stir the batter, pour a ladleful into the centre and spread quickly in circles from the middle out into a thin round. The batter should be loose enough to spread without dragging; add a spoon of water if it fights you.
  - Cook for about 2 minutes until the top looks dry and the edges lift. Ragi turns from wet brown to a matte, deeper brown when it is done. Spoon a quarter of the paneer podi down the middle, fold the dosa over, and slide it off. No need to flip a thin dosa.
  - Repeat to make four dosas, wiping the tawa with a damp cloth between each and re-oiling lightly. Serve two per person, hot, with the rest of the coriander on top.
notes: The batter keeps in the fridge for 2 days and thickens as it sits, so loosen with water before pouring. The filled dosas are fine for a tiffin box if wrapped in foil once cool; they go soft but stay tasty.
