swaps:
  - for: Yellow moong dal
    use: Whole green moong, soaked 6 hours, for the classic green pesarattu
    why: Traditional Andhra version, higher fibre, same grinding method
  - for: Ginger-garlic paste
    use: A knob of ginger ground with the dal
    why: No-garlic version; ginger is the traditional flavour anyway
  - for: Hung curd made at home
    use: Thick Greek-style curd from the tub
    why: Skips 2 hours of draining
steps:
  - Rinse all the moong dal and soak it for 4 hours (overnight is fine). If making hung curd at home, tie plain curd in a muslin cloth and hang it over a bowl for 2 hours at the same time.
  - Drain the dal and grind it in the mixie with one green chilli, the ginger-garlic paste, a pinch of cumin and salt, adding water a spoon at a time to get a smooth batter that pours like thick cream. No fermentation needed; rest it 10 minutes.
  - Chop the onion and the second green chilli finely and keep them in a bowl; they go on top of each pesarattu, not into the batter.
  - Heat a non-stick tawa on medium-high until a drop of water skitters, then wipe on a few drops of oil with a folded tissue. Pour a ladle of batter in the centre and spread outwards in a spiral into a thin round.
  - Scatter a spoonful of the onion and chilli over the wet surface and press lightly with the back of the ladle so they stick. Drizzle a few drops of oil around the rim and cook 2 to 3 minutes until the edges lift and the underside is golden brown.
  - Flip, press gently, and cook 1 minute more so the onion browns. Fold in half and slide onto a plate. Repeat; the batter makes about 6 pesarattu, 3 per person. Wipe the tawa with the oiled tissue between each.
  - Whisk the hung curd with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin and serve a katori alongside the hot pesarattu, with a spoon of ginger chutney if you have it.
notes: If the first pesarattu tears when flipped, the tawa is not hot enough or the batter is too thin; wait a minute and thicken with a spoon of besan. The batter keeps 2 days in the fridge. Pesarattu stays soft in a tiffin if wrapped in a cloth while warm; carry the hung curd separately.
