    why: Cuts the spice prep on a weeknight
steps:
  - Soak the tamarind in half a glass of warm water for 10 minutes, then squeeze and strain it, keeping the water and discarding the pulp.
  - Rinse the fish steaks, pat them dry and rub them with salt, turmeric and a pinch of red chilli powder. Leave them for 15 minutes while you cook the base.
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add a pinch of mustard seeds and wait for them to pop, then a pinch of methi seeds and a sprig of curry leaves. As soon as the methi turns light brown, add the chopped onion with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and golden, 5 minutes, adding a spoon of water if it sticks.
  - Add the tomato, coriander powder, red chilli powder and turmeric, and cook until the tomato collapses into a thick paste, about 4 minutes.
  - Pour in the tamarind water and a glass of hot water, add salt and bring the gravy to a rolling simmer. Let it bubble uncovered for 5 minutes until it turns from sharp-smelling to rounded and slightly thickened.
  - Lower the flame and slide the fish pieces in a single layer. Do not stir; instead tilt and swirl the kadhai to move the gravy over the fish. Cover and cook on low for 8 to 10 minutes until the flesh turns opaque and flakes when pressed at the thickest part.
  - Switch off and let the curry rest for 10 minutes; the fish firms up and the tang settles into the gravy. Finish with a little chopped coriander.
  - "This is a non-veg add-on: cook it separately and serve it in its own bowl beside the vegetarian lunch."
notes: Andhra fish curry is tangy and hot, so taste the gravy before the fish goes in and adjust chilli and tamarind then. It is better an hour later or the next day, and reheats gently without breaking if you do not stir it.
