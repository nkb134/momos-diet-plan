steps:
  - Put all the poha in a strainer and run water over it for about 20 seconds, no longer. Shake off the excess, sprinkle a pinch of salt and a pinch of turmeric over it and leave it to sit while you cook; the grains will soften on their own.
  - Steam all the moong sprouts in a small strainer set over a pot with a little boiling water, lid on, for 5 to 6 minutes. They should lose their raw bite but still snap when pressed. Set aside.
  - Heat a dry kadhai on medium and toast the peanuts for 2 to 3 minutes, shaking the pan, until they smell nutty and the skins darken a shade. Tip them out onto a plate.
  - Add the oil to the same kadhai. When it shimmers, drop in mustard seeds and wait for them to crackle, then add a pinch of cumin, a few curry leaves if you have them, and a slit green chilli.
  - Add the chopped onion and cook on medium for 3 to 4 minutes until it turns soft and translucent. If it starts to catch, splash in a spoon of water rather than more oil.
  - Stir in the steamed sprouts and the rest of the dry masalas (turmeric, a little red chilli, salt) and toss for a minute so the spices coat everything.
  - Fluff the soaked poha with a fork, add it to the kadhai with the toasted peanuts, and fold gently with a ladle. Cover, lower the heat, and let it steam for 2 minutes so the poha heats through and turns evenly yellow.
  - Switch off, squeeze the lemon over, and rest with the lid on for 2 minutes. Serve with the hung curd spooned on the side; a pinch of roasted cumin on the curd is nice.
notes: Over-soaked poha turns to mush, so rinse quickly and let it sit rather than soaking it in a bowl of water. Packs well for tiffin; carry the curd separately so the poha stays fluffy.
