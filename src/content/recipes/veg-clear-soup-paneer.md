swaps:
  - for: Button mushroom
    use: Chopped bottle gourd (lauki) or extra beans, same weight
    why: If mushrooms are not in the market
  - for: Ginger-garlic paste
    use: Julienned ginger only, 1 tbsp
    why: No-garlic households; the soup stays clean-tasting
  - for: Pan-seared paneer slabs
    use: Drop paneer cubes straight into the hot soup for the last 2 minutes
    why: One pot, fewer dishes
steps:
  - Cut the carrot into thin rounds, slice the beans on the diagonal, quarter the mushrooms and shred the cabbage. Keep them in separate piles since they go in one after another.
  - Heat half the oil in a pot on medium. Add the ginger-garlic paste and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant; keep it pale, do not brown it.
  - Pour in about 4 glasses of water, add salt, and bring to a boil. Add the carrot and beans and simmer, uncovered, for 5 minutes until the carrot is just tender.
  - Add the mushrooms and cook 3 minutes; they should turn darker and shrink a little. Then drop in the cabbage and cook 2 minutes more, no longer, so it keeps a slight crunch and the broth stays clear.
  - "Season with the dry masalas: plenty of crushed black pepper, a pinch of white pepper if you have it, and check salt. Switch off and leave covered while you do the paneer."
  - Pat the paneer slices dry and press coarsely crushed black pepper and a pinch of salt onto both faces. Heat the remaining oil on a non-stick tawa on medium-high and sear the slices for 1 to 2 minutes per side until golden and the pepper smells toasty. Do not go longer; low-fat paneer turns rubbery.
  - Ladle the soup into two deep bowls, add a few drops of lemon if you like, and serve the pepper paneer slabs on a small plate alongside, or lay them across the top of the soup to eat with a spoon.
notes: Keep the soup at a simmer rather than a hard boil so it stays clear. The broth keeps 2 days chilled and reheats well; sear the paneer fresh, since it stiffens when reheated.
