swaps:
  - for: Fresh methi leaves
    use: Kasuri methi, 3 tbsp, crushed, plus 1 katori chopped palak for bulk
    why: Fresh methi disappears in summer
  - for: Bajra flour
    use: Jowar or whole wheat atta, same weight
    why: Bajra is a winter grain; wheat rolls more easily for beginners
  - for: Hand-patted bajra roti
    use: Roll between two sheets of plastic or parchment
    why: Patting takes practice; rolling between sheets gives the same thin round
steps:
  - Pluck the methi leaves off the thick stems, wash in three changes of water to shift the grit, and chop roughly. Toss the leaves with a good pinch of salt and leave 10 minutes, then squeeze out the dark water; this takes most of the bitterness with it.
  - Cut the paneer into cubes. Chop the onion and tomato finely.
  - "Make the bajra dough while the methi rests: put the flour in a bowl with a pinch of salt, add hot (not boiling) water a spoon at a time and knead 3 to 4 minutes until it feels soft and smooth. Bajra has no gluten, so kneading warm is what holds it together. Cover with a damp cloth."
  - Heat all the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds first; when they crackle, add a pinch of hing and the onion. Cook 4 to 5 minutes with a splash of water until soft and light gold.
  - Add the tomato and the spice powders (turmeric, coriander, a little red chilli). Cook 3 minutes until the tomato melts into the onion.
  - Add the squeezed methi. It looks like a lot but wilts fast; stir on medium for 5 to 6 minutes until the leaves darken, shrink and lose their raw smell.
  - Fold in the paneer cubes and salt, cover and let it sit on low for 3 minutes so the paneer warms through and softens. Switch off and rest 5 minutes with the lid on.
  - For the rotis, divide the dough into 2 balls. Wet your palms, flatten a ball on a piece of plastic or directly on the board and pat outwards from the centre, turning as you go, into a round about as thick as a coin. Lift onto a hot tawa, cook 1 minute, flip when the underside shows spots, cook another minute, then hold over a direct flame with tongs for a few seconds so it puffs. Cut each roti in half; that is one serving.
notes: Bajra rotis stiffen as they cool, so eat them within 10 minutes of cooking. The methi paneer keeps a day in the fridge and reheats gently with a spoon of water; it is stronger tasting the next day.
