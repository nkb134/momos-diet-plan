swaps:
  - for: Brown rice
    use: Hand-pounded or parboiled rice, 2 whistles
    why: Cooks faster and is easier to find in some regions
  - for: Fresh bhindi
    use: Frozen cut bhindi, cooked straight from frozen on high heat
    why: No washing and drying; off-season option
  - for: Separate masala for the bhindi
    use: Cook the bhindi plain with just cumin, salt and turmeric
    why: Halves the chopping when time is short
steps:
  - Rinse the brown rice until the water runs clear and soak it 30 minutes. Wash the bhindi first thing and spread it on a cloth to dry completely; wet bhindi turns slimy in the pan. Once dry, trim the ends and cut into 1-inch pieces.
  - Drain the rice into the pressure cooker with two and a quarter times its volume of water and a pinch of salt. Cook high till the first whistle, then low for 3 whistles (about 15 minutes). Let the pressure drop naturally and leave the lid on another 5 minutes before fluffing.
  - Rinse the masoor dal (no soaking needed) and cook it in a saucepan with three times its volume of water, turmeric and salt. Bring to a boil, skim the foam, then simmer half-covered 15 to 18 minutes until the dal breaks down completely. Whisk it smooth with the ladle. If your cooker is free, 2 whistles does the same job.
  - For the dal tadka, heat a third of the oil in a small pan. Cumin seeds go in first; when they crackle, add half the chopped onion and cook until golden, then half the tomato and a pinch of the spice powders. Cook until the tomato softens, then pour the whole tempering into the dal, simmer 2 minutes and switch off.
  - For the bhindi, heat the remaining oil in a wide non-stick kadhai on medium-high. Add the bhindi in a single layer and leave it alone for 3 minutes before the first stir. Keep the lid off and stir only every couple of minutes; after 8 to 10 minutes the pieces should be bright green with browned edges and no strings.
  - Push the bhindi to one side, add the rest of the onion to the empty half with a splash of water, cook 3 minutes, then add the remaining tomato and spice powders and salt. Mix everything together for 2 more minutes so the masala clings to the bhindi.
  - Toast the paneer cubes on a dry non-stick tawa or the same kadhai for a minute each side and fold them into the bhindi at the end so they stay firm.
  - Serve the dal over the brown rice with the bhindi masala on the side and the thick curd in its own katori.
notes: Salt the bhindi only after it has browned, or it releases water and goes limp. The dal and rice reheat well for 2 days; the bhindi is best eaten the day it is made, though it survives a tiffin box if packed without the curd.
