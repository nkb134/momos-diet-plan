swaps:
  - for: Large bharta baingan
    use: 3 to 4 long baingan, roasted the same way
    why: Round bharta brinjal is not always in the market; long ones roast faster but taste smokier if a little charred
  - for: Flame-roasting the baingan
    use: Halve it, rub with a drop of oil and cook cut-side down on a covered tawa on low for 12 to 15 min
    why: Lower effort and no mess on the burner
  - for: Greek / thick curd
    use: Plain toned-milk curd hung in a muslin cloth for 2 hours
    why: Cheaper and the same thickness
steps:
  - Wash and dry the baingan, prick it in five or six places with a knife and rub a few drops of the oil over the skin. Set it directly on a medium gas flame and turn it with tongs every 2 to 3 minutes until the skin is blackened all over and the body collapses when pressed, about 12 to 15 minutes.
  - Drop the roasted baingan into a bowl, cover it and leave it for 5 minutes so the steam loosens the skin. Peel the charred skin away under a trickle of water, pull off the stem and mash the flesh with a fork. Keep any juice that collects.
  - While the baingan cools, chop the onion and tomato and cube the paneer. Whisk the curd smooth in a bowl, stir the ground flaxseed into it with a pinch of black salt, and refrigerate.
  - Heat the rest of the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds and let them sizzle for a few seconds, then add the chopped onion with a pinch of salt. Cook it until it turns soft and pale gold, adding a spoon of water whenever it starts to catch instead of more oil.
  - Add the tomato, the turmeric, red chilli and coriander powder from the masala mix and a splash of water. Cook on medium until the tomato breaks down and the masala looks glossy and pulls away from the pan, about 4 minutes.
  - Tip in the mashed baingan and mix well. Cook uncovered on medium-low for 6 to 8 minutes, stirring now and then, until the raw smell is gone and the bharta thickens and darkens slightly.
  - Fold in the paneer cubes, cover and let them warm through on low for 2 minutes. Finish with garam masala and take the pan off the heat.
  - Rest the bharta for 5 minutes with the lid on so the flavours settle, then serve hot with the chilled flaxseed curd on the side.
notes: The bharta tastes better the next day and keeps 2 days in the fridge; reheat it in a pan with a spoon of water. For a tiffin, pack the curd in a separate leak-proof box and stir the flaxseed in fresh so it does not thicken the curd too much.
