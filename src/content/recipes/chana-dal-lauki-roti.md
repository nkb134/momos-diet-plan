steps:
  - Rinse the chana dal until the water runs clear and soak it in warm water for at least 30 minutes, an hour if you have it. Meanwhile knead all the atta with a pinch of salt and enough water into a soft dough, cover and rest it.
  - Peel and dice the lauki into 2 cm pieces, chop the tomato and cube the paneer. Whisk the curd smooth with a pinch of roasted cumin and salt and keep it aside.
  - Drain the dal into the pressure cooker with the lauki, turmeric, a slit green chilli and 2.5 times its volume of water. Close the lid and cook on high until the first whistle, then on low for 3 more whistles. Let the pressure drop on its own; the dal should hold its shape but crush easily between two fingers.
  - Heat the oil in a kadhai on medium. Add a bay leaf, a pinch of cumin seeds, a small piece of cinnamon and one or two cloves from the masala mix and let them sizzle for 15 seconds. Add grated ginger and the chopped tomato with a splash of water and cook until the tomato collapses, about 3 minutes.
  - Pour the cooked dal and lauki into the kadhai, add the coriander and red chilli powder, salt and a pinch of sugar in the Bengali way. Simmer on low for 5 minutes, thinning with hot water if it gets too thick; cholar dal should be thick enough to sit on a roti.
  - Add the paneer cubes and the grated coconut, simmer 2 minutes more, sprinkle garam masala and switch off. Rest the dal covered while you make the rotis.
  - "Divide the dough into 4 balls, roll each into a thin round and cook on a hot tawa on medium-high: about 30 seconds until bubbles form, flip, 40 seconds until brown spots appear, then lift it onto the open flame with tongs for a few seconds to puff."
  - Serve two rotis per person with the dal and a katori of curd on the side.
notes: The dal thickens as it cools, so loosen it with a little hot water when reheating. Rotis and dal both travel well in a tiffin; wrap the rotis in a cloth while warm so they stay soft.
