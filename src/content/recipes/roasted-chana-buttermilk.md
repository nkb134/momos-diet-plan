swaps:

    use: Store-bought bhuna chana, about 1 katori
    why: Zero-effort version; just toss with the masala
  - for: Buttermilk
    use: 1 katori curd whisked with 2 glasses of cold water
    why: Makes chaas from what is already in the fridge
  - for: Peanuts
    use: Roasted makhana, a small mutthi
    why: Lower fat and lighter on the stomach
steps:
  - Soak all the kala chana overnight, drain, and pressure cook with fresh water and a pinch of salt for 4 to 5 whistles. Let the pressure release, then drain completely and spread the chana on a plate or cloth for 10 minutes to dry; wet chana will steam instead of roasting.
  - Heat a dry kadhai (non-stick or well seasoned) on medium-low. Add the drained chana and the peanuts and roast, stirring every minute, for 10 to 12 minutes. The chana skins will wrinkle and the beans turn firm and crisp at the edges; the peanuts should smell toasty.
  - Switch off, sprinkle over the dry masalas (chaat masala, a pinch of red chilli, black salt) and toss while hot so the spices stick. Spread out to cool; they crisp further as they cool.
  - For the buttermilk, pour it into a bowl and whisk for a minute to make it frothy. Add roasted cumin powder, black salt, a pinch of black pepper and half the chopped coriander.
  - If you have a few mint leaves or a small piece of ginger, crush them and stir in; leave the buttermilk in the fridge for 10 minutes so it is properly cold and the flavours settle.
  - Serve the crunchy chana in a bowl with the remaining coriander over it, and a tall glass of masala buttermilk next to it. Eat the chana by the handful and drink the chaas alongside.
notes: The roasted chana keeps crisp for 3 to 4 days in an airtight box, so make a bigger batch and portion it for snacks. Pack the buttermilk in a bottle and keep it cold; it separates a little but a shake fixes that.
