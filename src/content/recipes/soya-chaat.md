steps:
  - Bring a saucepan of water to a boil with a pinch of salt and turmeric. Drop in all the soya chunks and boil for 5 minutes until they double in size and turn soft all the way through.
  - Drain, rinse under cold water, then squeeze the chunks firmly between your palms to press out the water; this removes the beany smell. Cut any big chunks in half.
  - Heat a dry non-stick kadhai on medium-high. Add the squeezed soya and dry-roast for 4 to 5 minutes, tossing often, until the surfaces are dry and lightly browned at the edges. There is no oil in this recipe; the pan does the work.
  - Sprinkle over most of the dry masalas (chaat masala, roasted cumin, red chilli, black salt) while the pan is still hot and toss for 30 seconds so the spices stick. Tip into a bowl and let cool for 5 minutes.
  - Add the chopped onion, tomato and cucumber, squeeze the lemon over, and toss. Taste for salt; chaat masala already carries some.
  - Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt and the remaining masala until smooth. Either spoon it over the chaat like a dahi chaat or serve it as a thick dip on the side. Eat straight away while the soya is still warm against the cold curd.
notes: Squeeze the soya well or the chaat goes watery within minutes. If carrying to work, pack the roasted soya, the chopped veg and the curd in three boxes and mix at the desk.
