swaps:
  - for: Fresh mint for the curd
    use: Half a teaspoon of dried mint or just extra coriander
    why: Mint is only used for the hung curd dip and is easy to skip off-season
  - for: Hung curd made at home
    use: Ready thick curd or Greek curd straight from the pack
    why: Saves the 2-hour hanging step on a busy morning
  - for: Capsicum
    use: Grated carrot or chopped palak
    why: Any quick-cooking vegetable works in the batter
steps:
  - If the hung curd is not ready, tie plain curd in a muslin cloth and hang it over a bowl for at least 2 hours, or overnight in the fridge. Whisk it smooth, stir in chopped coriander, a few torn mint leaves if you have them, black salt and a little roasted cumin from the masala mix, and keep it cold.
  - Chop the onion, capsicum and tomato finely and keep the remaining coriander chopped. Squeeze the tomato pieces lightly so they do not water down the batter.
  - In a bowl, whisk all the besan with a pinch of turmeric, red chilli powder, ajwain, salt and enough water to make a pourable batter the thickness of dosa batter, with no lumps. Rest it for 5 minutes so the besan absorbs the water.
  - Stir the chopped vegetables and coriander into the batter just before cooking. Add a spoon more water if it has thickened while resting.
  - Heat a non-stick tawa on medium until a drop of water skips across it. Wipe on a few drops of the oil with a folded tissue or a piece of onion.
  - Pour a ladle of batter into the centre and spread it outward in circles with the back of the ladle to a thin round. Cook on medium for about 2 minutes until the top looks dry and small holes appear, then flip.
  - Cook the second side for 1 to 2 minutes until golden spots appear. Repeat with the rest of the batter, wiping the tawa with oil only between chillas; the batter makes about 4 medium chillas.
  - Serve two chillas per person hot off the tawa with the hung curd and mint dip on the side.
notes: The batter should be spread in one go; if it drags, the tawa is too hot, so sprinkle water on it and wipe. Chillas stay soft for a tiffin if wrapped in a cloth while warm; pack the curd separately.
