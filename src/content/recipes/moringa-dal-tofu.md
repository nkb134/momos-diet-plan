swaps:
  - for: Fresh moringa leaves
    use: Palak or methi, 2 katori chopped, or 1 tbsp moringa powder stirred in at the end
    why: Moringa trees are seasonal and not every market carries the leaves
  - for: Fresh grated coconut
    use: Desiccated coconut, 1.5 tbsp
    why: Pantry-stable option
  - for: Firm tofu
    use: Crumbled low-fat paneer
    why: Same poriyal method, easier to find
steps:
  - Strip the moringa leaves off their stalks by running your fingers down each stem against the direction of growth; discard the stems. Rinse the leaves in a bowl of water and drain in a strainer. Press the tofu in a muslin cloth for 10 minutes, then crumble it coarsely with your fingers.
  - Rinse all the moong dal and put it in the pressure cooker with three times its volume of water, a pinch of turmeric and a few drops of the oil to stop it frothing. Cook on high till the first whistle, then low for 2 whistles. Release naturally and mash lightly.
  - Return the cooker to a low flame, add the moringa leaves and salt, and simmer uncovered 5 to 6 minutes until the leaves are tender and the dal has turned a deep green. Do not overcook or the leaves go bitter.
  - For the dal tempering, heat a third of the oil in a small pan. Mustard seeds first; when they stop popping, add cumin, a broken dry red chilli, a pinch of hing and curry leaves. Pour over the dal, stir, and switch off. Squeeze in a little lemon if you have one.
  - For the poriyal, heat the remaining oil in a kadhai on medium. Mustard seeds first again, then a pinch of urad dal if you keep it, curry leaves and a chopped green chilli or a pinch of chilli powder from the mix.
  - Add the crumbled tofu with turmeric and salt. Stir on medium-high for 5 to 6 minutes; the crumbs should dry out and pick up light brown edges. Splash a spoon of water if it sticks rather than adding oil.
  - Switch off, fold in the grated coconut and let it warm through in the residual heat for a minute. The coconut should stay white and sweet.
  - Serve the moringa dal in bowls with the tofu poriyal on the side and the thick curd in a katori. Rest the dal 5 minutes off the heat; it thickens and the flavours settle.
notes: Moringa leaves cook in minutes, so add them only after the dal is done. The dal keeps 2 days chilled; the poriyal is best fresh but reheats fine on a dry tawa. Both pack well for tiffin with the curd in its own cup.
