swaps:
  - for: Button mushrooms
    use: Grated lauki, squeezed dry, or extra capsicum
    why: Mushrooms are perishable; use what is in the fridge
  - for: Finely chopping the mushrooms by hand
    use: Pulse them 3 or 4 times in the mixie
    why: Faster on a weeknight; do not over-pulse or they turn to paste
  - for: Ground flaxseed
    use: Chia seeds, same amount
    why: Either works sprinkled at the end
steps:
  - Wipe the mushrooms clean with a damp cloth and chop them small, about pea-sized. Chop the onion, tomato and capsicum finely so they cook in the same time. Crumble the paneer with your fingers into small, uneven bits.
  - Heat a non-stick kadhai on high without oil and add the mushrooms. Stir for 4 to 5 minutes until they release their water and then go dry and start to brown at the edges. Tip them onto a plate.
  - Turn the heat to medium, add all the oil, and drop in cumin seeds. When they sizzle and darken a shade, add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook 3 to 4 minutes until translucent, adding a spoon of water if it sticks.
  - Add the capsicum and cook 2 minutes so it softens but keeps a little bite. Add the tomato and the spice powders (turmeric, chilli, coriander, a pinch of garam masala) and cook 3 minutes until the tomato breaks down and the masala looks thick.
  - Return the mushrooms and add the crumbled paneer. Fold gently on medium for 3 minutes, just long enough for the paneer to heat through and pick up the colour of the masala. Overcooking dries it out.
  - Switch off. Season with salt, sprinkle the ground flaxseed and the chopped coriander over the top, and give it one final toss. Rest 2 minutes before serving so the paneer softens again in the steam.
notes: Add salt only after the paneer goes in; salting the mushrooms early makes them weep. Eat within a day; in a tiffin it stays soft if you pack it while warm and add the coriander at the end. Two leftover rotis or a toast alongside is enough for a light dinner.
