swaps:
  - for: Rajma soaked overnight
    use: Tinned or pre-boiled rajma, about 1½ katori drained
    why: Forgot to soak; add at step 5 and simmer 15 minutes
  - for: Brown rice
    use: Hand-pounded or parboiled rice, same weight
    why: Cooks faster and is easier to find in some areas
  - for: Greek curd
    use: Regular thick curd, same weight
    why: Cheaper; the raita will be a little thinner
steps:
  - Soak all the rajma in plenty of water for 8 hours or overnight; the beans should be plump and wrinkle-free. Soak the brown rice separately for 30 minutes while you prep the rest.
  - Drain the rajma, put it in the pressure cooker with fresh water to cover by two fingers, a pinch of salt and a bay leaf. Cook on high until the first whistle, then lower the flame and cook 20 minutes more (about 5 to 6 whistles). Let the pressure drop on its own. A bean should crush easily between two fingers; if not, give it 2 more whistles.
  - Meanwhile cook the drained rice in a saucepan with 2½ times its volume of water, covered, on low, for 25 to 30 minutes until the grains are tender and the water is gone. Rest, covered, off the heat.
  - Heat the oil in a kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds; when they sizzle and darken, add the chopped onion and cook 6 to 8 minutes until deep golden, adding a splash of water whenever it looks like sticking.
  - Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook a minute until the raw smell goes. Add the tomato puree and the dry masalas (coriander, turmeric, red chilli, a little garam masala, salt). Cook, stirring, for 5 to 6 minutes until the masala thickens and oil-free bubbles form at the edges.
  - Pour in the cooked rajma with its cooking water. Mash a ladleful of beans against the side of the kadhai to thicken the gravy, then simmer uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the gravy is thick and coats the beans.
  - For the raita, whisk the curd until smooth with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin, then fold in the grated cucumber. Keep cold.
  - Grate the paneer over the rajma just before serving so it melts a little into the gravy. Serve the rajma over the brown rice with the raita alongside.
notes: Rajma tastes better the next day, so the curry can be made ahead and kept 2 days in the fridge; reheat with a splash of water. Old rajma stock refuses to soften; if it stays hard after 8 whistles, the beans are the problem, not you.
