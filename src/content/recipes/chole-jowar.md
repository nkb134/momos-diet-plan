steps:
  - Soak the kabuli chana in plenty of water for 8 hours or overnight. Drain, put it in the pressure cooker with fresh water to cover by two fingers, a pinch of salt and a small piece of cinnamon. Cook on high to the first whistle, then low for 5 to 6 whistles. Let the pressure drop naturally; the chana should mash easily under a spoon. Keep the cooking water.
  - While the chana cooks, chop the onion and tomato finely. Slice the cucumber, cut the carrot into sticks and grate the paneer. Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin and refrigerate.
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds and a bay leaf, and when the cumin darkens add the onion with a pinch of salt. Cook it until deep golden, 7 to 8 minutes, adding a spoon of water each time it catches at the bottom rather than more oil.
  - Add grated ginger and the tomato, along with turmeric, coriander powder, red chilli and a pinch of ajwain from the masala mix. Cook on medium until the tomato is fully soft and the masala looks dark and jammy, about 5 minutes.
  - Tip in the cooked chana with a glass of its cooking water. Crush a handful of chana against the side of the pan with the ladle to thicken the gravy. Simmer uncovered on low for 12 to 15 minutes until the gravy turns thick and coats the chana; finish with garam masala and a pinch of amchur.
  - For the jowar rotis, bring a little over half a glass of water to a boil, pour it over the jowar flour with a pinch of salt and mix with a spoon. When cool enough to handle, knead for 2 minutes into a smooth, pliable dough; it must be warm and soft or it will crack. Divide into 4 balls.
  - Dust a ball with jowar flour and pat it out with your palm on a plastic sheet or the chopping board to a thin round, turning it as you go. Slide it onto a hot tawa on medium-high, wet the top surface with your fingers, and cook 40 seconds; flip and cook until spots appear, then lift it onto the flame with tongs for a few seconds to puff.
  - Serve two rotis per person with the chole topped with grated paneer, the cucumber and carrot salad with a squeeze of lemon and black salt, and a katori of curd.
notes: Chole tastes better the next day and freezes well, so this is a good make-ahead lunch. Jowar rotis stiffen as they cool; eat them fresh, or wrap them in a cloth immediately for a tiffin.
