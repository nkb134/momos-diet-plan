swaps:
  - for: Chicken breast
    use: Boneless thigh, same weight
    why: Stays juicier if you tend to overcook; slightly more fat
  - for: Hung curd
    use: Thick curd stirred with 1 tsp besan
    why: No time to hang curd; the besan keeps the marinade from sliding off
  - for: Grill pan
    use: Ordinary tawa or an airfryer at 200°C for 12 minutes
    why: Whatever you own
steps:
  - Cut the chicken breast into chunks of roughly equal size, about two fingers wide, so they cook at the same rate. Make a couple of shallow slashes in each piece.
  - In a bowl, mix the hung curd, ginger-garlic paste, the dry masalas (kashmiri red chilli for colour, coriander, cumin, garam masala, a pinch of turmeric, crushed kasuri methi, salt) and half the lemon juice. Add the chicken and rub the marinade into the cuts.
  - Cover and rest for at least 30 minutes; a few hours or overnight in the fridge gives a deeper flavour and a more tender bite. Take it out 10 minutes before cooking so it is not fridge-cold.
  - Heat a grill pan or a heavy tawa on high until a drop of water skids across it. Brush the oil over the pan surface with a spoon or folded paper; there is very little, and the marinade will keep the pieces from sticking.
  - Lay the chicken pieces down with space between them. Leave them alone for 4 minutes so a dark char forms, then turn with tongs and cook the other side for 4 to 5 minutes. Turn the flame to medium if the surface blackens before the inside cooks.
  - "Check doneness: press a piece and it should feel firm and spring back, and the juices from a cut should run clear with no pink inside. Thick pieces may need 2 minutes extra on their edges."
  - "Rest on a plate for 3 minutes, then squeeze the remaining lemon over. Serve alongside the vegetarian meal of the day as a separate side; this add-on is cooked on its own and is not stirred into the dal or sabzi. Optional: thread the pieces onto skewers and grill under an oven at 220°C for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once."
notes: Dry the pan-cooked chicken on high heat rather than covering it, or it stews in its own juices. Cooked tikka keeps 2 days chilled and is good cold in a tiffin with sliced onion and lemon.
