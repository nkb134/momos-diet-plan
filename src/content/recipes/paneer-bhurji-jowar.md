swaps:
  - for: Jowar flour
    use: Whole wheat atta or bajra flour, same weight
    why: Whatever is in the tin; wheat rolls easiest
  - for: Ginger-garlic paste
    use: A pinch of grated ginger and a pinch of hing
    why: No-garlic version
  - for: Hand-patted jowar rotis
    use: Two leftover rotis warmed on the tawa
    why: Breakfast in 15 minutes
steps:
  - "Start with the dough so it rests while you cook: put the jowar flour in a bowl with a pinch of salt, add hot water a spoon at a time and knead 3 to 4 minutes until smooth. Cover with a damp cloth."
  - Chop the onion, tomato and capsicum finely. Crumble the paneer with your fingers into small bits.
  - Heat all the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds; when they darken and sizzle, add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook 4 minutes until soft and pale gold, splashing a spoon of water if needed.
  - Add the ginger-garlic paste and stir 30 seconds until the raw smell disappears. Add the capsicum and cook 2 minutes, then the tomato and the spice powders (turmeric, chilli, coriander, a pinch of garam masala). Cook 3 minutes until the tomato breaks down.
  - Add the crumbled paneer and salt. Fold gently on medium for 2 to 3 minutes, just until it is hot through and stained with the masala. Switch off and cover; it stays soft in the steam while you make the rotis.
  - Divide the dough into 4 balls. Wet your palms and pat each into a small round about the thickness of a coin, on plastic or the board. Cook on a hot tawa 1 minute, flip when spots appear, 1 minute more, then hold over the flame with tongs a few seconds so it puffs.
  - Serve two rotis per person with the bhurji, a squeeze of lemon and chopped coriander if you have it.
notes: Jowar rotis harden as they cool, so eat them straight off the tawa; for tiffin, wrap them in a cloth while hot. The bhurji keeps a day in the fridge and reheats with a spoon of water.
