swaps:
  - for: Dried lobia
    use: Rajma or kabuli chana, soaked overnight, 6 to 7 whistles
    why: If lobia is not in the pantry, the same masala carries any bean
  - for: Raw beetroot in the salad
    use: Grated carrot
    why: No red-stained chopping board on a weeknight
  - for: Fresh rotis
    use: Two rotis left from breakfast, warmed on the tawa
    why: Saves 10 minutes of rolling
steps:
  - "Soak all the lobia in plenty of water for 4 to 6 hours (or 1 hour in hot water if you forgot). Drain, add fresh water to cover by two fingers, a pinch of salt and turmeric, and pressure cook: high till the first whistle, then low for 3 whistles, about 12 minutes. Release naturally. The beans should be soft with the skins intact."
  - While the beans cook, knead the atta with a pinch of salt and just enough water to make a soft, smooth dough. Cover and rest it 15 minutes; rested dough rolls thin without tearing.
  - Chop the onion and tomato finely. Slice the cucumber, grate the beetroot on the coarse side of the grater, and cut the paneer into small cubes. Put the salad together on a plate and keep it in the fridge.
  - Heat all the oil in the kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds first; when they sizzle and darken a shade, add the chopped onion with a pinch of salt and cook 5 to 6 minutes until soft and golden at the edges, splashing a spoon of water if it sticks.
  - Add a spoon of ginger-garlic paste if you keep it, then the tomato and the spice powders (turmeric, coriander, chilli, a little garam masala from the mix). Cook 5 minutes on medium-low until the tomato collapses and the masala looks glossy and pulls together, not watery.
  - Tip in the cooked lobia with its cooking water. Simmer uncovered 8 to 10 minutes, mashing a few beans with the back of the ladle so the gravy thickens. It should coat a spoon. Check salt and switch off.
  - "Divide the dough into 4 balls. Roll each thin on a lightly floured board and cook on a hot tawa: 30 seconds on the first side until bubbles appear, flip, 45 seconds until brown spots, then flip once more and press gently with a cloth or tongs so it puffs. No oil or ghee needed."
  - Serve the lobia masala with the rotis, the cucumber-beetroot-paneer salad, and the thick curd in a separate katori. A pinch of chaat masala over the salad is a good idea.
notes: Lobia masala tastes better the next day and freezes well for a month; make double. For tiffin, pack the salad and curd separately and keep the beetroot away from the paneer or everything turns pink.
