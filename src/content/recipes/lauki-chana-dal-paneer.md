swaps:
  - for: Lauki
    use: Pumpkin, tinda or raw papaya, same weight
    why: Any soft gourd cooks into the dal the same way
  - for: Grilling paneer slabs on the tawa
    use: Cube the paneer and stir it straight into the dal in the last 2 minutes
    why: One pan less on a tired evening
  - for: Chana dal
    use: Moong dal with only 2 whistles
    why: Faster and lighter for dinner
steps:
  - Rinse the chana dal and soak it in warm water for 30 minutes. Peel and cube the lauki, chop the tomato and coriander, and cut the paneer into slabs about 1.5 cm thick.
  - Drain the dal into the pressure cooker with the lauki, turmeric, a slit green chilli, a pinch of hing and 2.5 times its volume of water. Cook on high to the first whistle, then low for 3 more whistles. Let the pressure release on its own; the dal should be soft but not mushy and the lauki translucent.
  - Heat half the oil in a kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds and a bay leaf, and when the cumin darkens add grated ginger and the chopped tomato with a splash of water. Cook until the tomato collapses, about 3 minutes.
  - Pour in the cooked dal and lauki, add salt, coriander powder and a pinch of sugar, and simmer on low for 5 to 6 minutes until the dal thickens slightly and the tomato has disappeared into it. Finish with garam masala and half the coriander.
  - Meanwhile rub the paneer slabs with the remaining spice mix, salt and the rest of the oil. Heat a tawa on medium-high and grill the slabs 2 to 3 minutes per side, turning once with tongs, until golden with brown edges.
  - Rest the dal covered for 5 minutes. Serve it in bowls with the grilled paneer slabs laid on top and the remaining coriander scattered over.
notes: The dal thickens overnight; loosen with hot water when reheating and it is just as good the next day. Grill the paneer fresh if you can, since it turns firm after a night in the fridge.
