swaps:
  - for: Fresh grated coconut ground to milk
    use: 2 tbsp thin coconut milk from a tetra pack
    why: Skips the grinding and straining
  - for: Chicken breast
    use: Boneless thigh, same weight, simmered 5 minutes longer
    why: Stays juicier if you tend to overcook breast
  - for: Carrot
    use: Beans or green peas
    why: Any firm vegetable that takes 10 minutes to soften
steps:
  - Cut the chicken breast into even 2 cm chunks and rub them with salt, a pinch of turmeric and a good pinch of crushed black pepper. Leave them while you do the rest.
  - Grind the grated coconut with half a glass of warm water in the mixie for a minute, then squeeze it through a strainer into a bowl; this thin coconut milk goes in at the end.
  - Heat the oil in a small pan on medium. Add a crushed cardamom, a clove and a small piece of cinnamon from the masala mix and let them sizzle for 10 seconds, then add the sliced onion with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and translucent, not brown, about 3 minutes with a splash of water if it sticks.
  - Add a little grated ginger, a slit green chilli and a few curry leaves, stir for 30 seconds, then add the carrot and the chicken. Toss for a minute until the chicken turns white on the outside.
  - Pour in a glass of hot water, bring it to a simmer, cover and cook on low for 10 to 12 minutes until the carrot is tender and the chicken is cooked through with no pink at the centre of the thickest piece.
  - Lower the flame to the minimum, pour in the coconut milk and warm it for a minute without letting it boil, or it will split. Check salt and finish with more crushed pepper.
  - "Rest the stew for 5 minutes off the heat. This is a non-veg add-on: cook it in its own pan and serve it in a separate bowl beside the vegetarian dinner."
notes: The stew should be thin and pale, peppery rather than chilli-hot. It keeps a day in the fridge; reheat gently and never boil once the coconut milk is in.
