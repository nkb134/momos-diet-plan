steps:
  - Rinse all the toor dal and soak it in warm water for 20 minutes. Soak the brown rice in a separate bowl at the same time. Drop the tamarind into half a glass of hot water to soften.
  - Put the drained dal in the pressure cooker with about 3 times its volume of water, a pinch of turmeric and a few drops of the oil. Cook for 3 whistles on high, then lower the flame for 5 minutes and switch off. Once the pressure drops, mash the dal with the back of a ladle until creamy.
  - Cook the drained rice in a saucepan with 2½ times its volume of water, covered, on low, for 25 to 30 minutes. Rest off the heat, lid on.
  - Squeeze the tamarind well and strain the water into a pot. Add the drumstick pieces, half the dry masalas (sambar powder, a pinch of turmeric, salt) and a glass of water. Simmer for 10 minutes until the drumstick is tender and you can scrape the flesh from the skin with a spoon.
  - Pour the mashed dal into the tamarind pot, thin with water to a pourable consistency, and simmer 5 minutes. Add the paneer cubes for the last 2 minutes so they soak up the sambar without going tough.
  - "Temper: heat half the oil in a small pan, add mustard seeds and wait until they pop, then a pinch of hing, a dry red chilli and curry leaves. Pour this over the sambar and cover immediately so the aroma stays in."
  - For the poriyal, heat the remaining oil in the kadhai, crackle mustard seeds, then add a spoon of urad dal and let it turn golden. Add the chopped beans with salt, a pinch of turmeric and 2 tbsp of water. Cover and cook on low for 8 to 10 minutes until the beans are tender but still bright. Uncover, add the grated coconut, and toss for a minute on high to dry it out.
  - Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt until smooth. Serve the rice with sambar ladled over it, the poriyal on the side, and the curd in a small bowl to finish the meal.
notes: Sambar thickens as it sits; loosen with hot water when reheating. Everything travels well in a three-tier tiffin, with the curd in its own sealed box.
