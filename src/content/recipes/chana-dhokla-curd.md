swaps:
  - for: Overnight-fermented batter
    use: Grind and rest the batter 30 minutes, then add a teaspoon of fruit salt just before steaming
    why: Same-day dhokla with less planning; the taste is milder
  - for: Chana dal
    use: Equal weight of besan whisked with the curd and water
    why: No soaking or grinding, a 15-minute version
  - for: Dhokla plate
    use: A steel thali or cake tin set on a stand inside the pressure cooker
    why: Any flat dish that fits the cooker works
steps:
  - Soak the chana dal in plenty of water for 4 to 5 hours. Drain and grind it in the mixie with about half the toned curd and just enough water to make a thick batter that falls from the spoon in a heavy ribbon; keep it slightly grainy rather than silky.
  - Stir in salt, turmeric, a pinch of hing and one crushed green chilli. Cover the batter and leave it in a warm corner for 6 to 8 hours or overnight until it smells faintly sour and has risen a little.
  - When ready to steam, put 2 glasses of water in the pressure cooker (lid without the whistle, or use a wide steamer) and bring it to a boil. Grease the dhokla plate with a few drops of the oil.
  - Give the batter a gentle stir, add a pinch of baking soda or half a teaspoon of fruit salt with a spoon of water and fold it in briskly until it turns airy. Pour immediately into the plate to about 2 cm depth.
  - Steam on medium heat for 15 to 18 minutes; a knife pushed into the centre should come out clean. Take the plate out and let the dhokla cool for 5 minutes before cutting it into squares.
  - For the tempering, heat the remaining oil in a small pan on medium. Add mustard seeds and wait for them to crackle, then the sesame seeds, the second green chilli slit, a few curry leaves and a pinch of hing. After 10 seconds add 3 tbsp of water and a pinch of sugar, let it bubble for a few seconds and spoon it evenly over the squares.
  - Whisk the hung curd with the chopped coriander, a pinch of salt and a little cumin powder for a dip. Whisk the leftover toned curd with the ground flaxseed and a pinch of black salt to serve as a bowl on the side.
  - Serve the dhokla warm, with the coriander hung curd for dipping and the flaxseed curd alongside.
notes: Do not open the steamer in the first 10 minutes or the dhokla will sink. It keeps a day at room temperature and 3 days in the fridge; it is an excellent tiffin, just pack both curds separately.
