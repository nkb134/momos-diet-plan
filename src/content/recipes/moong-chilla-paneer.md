swaps:
  - for: Soaked and ground moong dal
    use: Moong dal flour (or besan), 3/4 katori, whisked with water to a pouring batter
    why: No soaking or grinding; batter is ready in 5 minutes
  - for: Palak in the stuffing
    use: Grated carrot or chopped capsicum
    why: Whatever is in the fridge
  - for: Fresh paneer stuffing
    use: Plain crumbled paneer with salt and chaat masala, no cooking
    why: Skips the stuffing pan on a rushed morning
steps:
  - Rinse all the moong dal and soak it 3 to 4 hours (overnight is fine). Drain and grind in the mixie with one green chilli, a pinch of salt and just enough water to make a smooth batter that pours like thick cream, a little thinner than dosa batter. Rest it 10 minutes.
  - While the batter rests, make the stuffing. Chop the onion, tomato, second green chilli and coriander; shred the palak. Crumble the paneer with your fingers.
  - Heat a third of the oil in a small pan on medium. Add cumin seeds; once they sizzle, add the onion and chilli and cook 3 minutes until translucent, using a spoon of water instead of more oil if it catches.
  - Add the tomato and the spice powders, cook 2 minutes, then the palak just until it wilts. Switch off, fold in the crumbled paneer, salt and half the coriander. The stuffing should be moist, not wet.
  - Heat a non-stick tawa on medium until a drop of water skitters across it. Wipe on a few drops of oil with a folded tissue. Pour a ladle of batter in the centre and spread outwards in a spiral into a thin round, the way you would a dosa.
  - Let the chilla cook untouched for 2 minutes until the edges lift and the top looks dry and matte. Drizzle a few drops of oil around the rim, flip, and cook 1 minute more until the second side has golden spots.
  - Flip back, spoon a share of the stuffing over one half, fold the other half over it and press lightly. Slide onto a plate. Repeat; the batter makes about 6 chillas, 3 per person, with the stuffing shared out evenly.
  - Serve hot with the rest of the coriander on top and a spoon of green chutney or plain curd on the side if you have it.
notes: If the first chilla sticks, the tawa is not hot enough; wait another minute and wipe again with oil. The batter and stuffing both keep a day in the fridge, so grind the night before. Chillas stay soft in a tiffin if wrapped in a cloth while still warm.
