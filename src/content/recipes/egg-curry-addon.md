steps:
  - "Boil the eggs in a saucepan: cover with cold water, bring to a boil, then simmer 9 minutes. Cool them in cold water for 5 minutes, peel, and score each egg lightly in two places so the gravy gets in. Rub with a pinch of turmeric and salt."
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium and roll the eggs in it for 2 minutes until the skin blisters and turns pale gold in spots. Lift them out onto a plate.
  - In the same kadhai add a pinch of cumin seeds and a bay leaf. When the cumin darkens, add the chopped onion with a pinch of salt and cook until golden, about 6 minutes, splashing in a spoon of water whenever it starts to catch.
  - Add the ginger-garlic paste and stir for a minute until the raw smell goes. Add the tomato with turmeric, red chilli powder and cumin powder from the masala mix, and cook on medium until the tomato melts and the masala darkens and releases a little oil at the edges, 4 to 5 minutes.
  - Pour in three-quarters of a glass of hot water and salt, bring it to a simmer and return the eggs. Cover and cook on low for 6 to 8 minutes until the gravy is medium-thick and coats the back of a spoon.
  - Finish with a pinch of garam masala and a little chopped coriander if you have it. Rest for 5 minutes off the flame.
  - "This is a non-veg add-on: cook it in its own pan and serve it in a separate bowl beside the vegetarian lunch."
notes: Bengali dimer jhol is a thin, homely gravy, not a rich one, so do not reduce it too far. It keeps 2 days in the fridge and reheats well, which makes it a practical tiffin add-on.
