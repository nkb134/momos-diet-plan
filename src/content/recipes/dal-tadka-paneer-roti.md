steps:
  - Rinse the toor dal and soak it for 15 minutes. Knead all the atta with a pinch of salt and enough water into a soft dough, cover and rest it. Cut the capsicum into strips, chop the onion and tomato, and cube the paneer.
  - "Cook the drained dal in the pressure cooker with turmeric, a pinch of hing, half the chopped tomato and 3 times its volume of water: high to the first whistle, then low for 3 to 4 whistles. Let the pressure drop naturally and whisk the dal smooth with a ladle; add salt and thin with hot water to a pouring consistency."
  - For the tadka, heat a third of the oil in a small pan on medium. Add cumin seeds, wait for them to sizzle and darken, then add a dry red chilli, grated ginger and the chopped half of the onion with a pinch of salt. Cook until the onion is golden, using a spoon of water when it sticks, then add a pinch of red chilli powder and immediately pour the tadka over the dal. Cover and let it sit.
  - For the paneer sabzi, heat the remaining oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium-high. Add the sliced onion and cook for 2 minutes, then the capsicum with salt and toss for 3 minutes until it blisters at the edges but stays crisp.
  - Add the rest of the tomato, turmeric, coriander powder and a pinch of kasuri methi if you have it. Cook for 2 minutes with a splash of water, then add the paneer and toss gently for 2 minutes so the cubes are coated and warm. Sprinkle garam masala and switch off.
  - "Divide the dough into 4 balls, roll each into a thin round and cook on a hot tawa on medium-high: 30 seconds until bubbles rise, flip, 40 seconds until brown spots form, then puff briefly on the open flame with tongs."
  - Serve two rotis per person with a bowl of the dal, the paneer sabzi and a katori of curd.
notes: Pour the tadka on just before serving if you can; the crackle and smell are half the dish. Everything except the curd travels well in a tiffin; pack the dal in a leak-proof box and loosen it with water when reheating.
