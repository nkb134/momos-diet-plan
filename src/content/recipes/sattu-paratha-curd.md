swaps:

    use: Roasted chana dal ground fine in the mixie, same weight

  - for: Stuffed paratha
    use: Knead sattu, onion and spices straight into the atta dough and roll plain
    why: Faster on a weekday; no stuffing to leak
  - for: Fresh onion in the filling
    use: Finely chopped green chilli and extra coriander
    why: No-onion households
steps:
  - Mix all the atta with a pinch of salt and enough water to make a soft, pliable dough, softer than you would for roti. Knead for 3 to 4 minutes, rub a drop of the oil on the surface, cover, and rest 15 minutes.
  - For the filling, put all the sattu in a bowl with the chopped onion, the dry masalas (ajwain, kalonji, red chilli powder, salt), a little chopped green chilli and coriander if you have them, and the lemon juice. Add a few drops of the oil and a spoon of water at a time, mixing with your fingers until the sattu holds together when pressed but is still crumbly. Too wet and it will leak; too dry and it will pour out when you bite.
  - Divide the dough into four balls and the filling into four portions. Flatten a ball into a small cup, put in one portion of filling, gather the edges over the top and pinch shut. Press flat with your palm.
  - Dust lightly with atta and roll gently from the centre outwards into a round about the size of your palm spread wide. Roll evenly so the filling does not push through in one spot.
  - Heat the tawa on medium-high. Lay a paratha on it and cook for about a minute until small bubbles appear, then flip. Brush a few drops of oil over the top, flip again, and press gently with a cloth or spatula so it puffs and browns in spots.
  - Cook until both sides have golden-brown patches and the paratha smells toasty, about 3 minutes in all. Repeat for the remaining three; the flour makes four parathas, two per person.
  - Whisk the curd until smooth with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin. Serve the hot parathas with the curd, a wedge of lemon and some sliced onion or green chilli on the side.
notes: Sattu parathas dry out as they sit, so eat them fresh or wrap them in a cloth inside the tiffin. The filling can be mixed the night before but add the onion only in the morning or it turns watery.
