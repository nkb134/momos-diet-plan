swaps:

    use: Kasuri methi, 3 tbsp, crushed and soaked in a spoon of water
    why: Off-season; the thepla will be milder
  - for: Fresh methi leaves
    use: Chopped palak or grated lauki, same weight
    why: Different green, same method; squeeze lauki dry first
  - for: Rolled thepla
    use: Press the dough flat with your palm on the tawa
    why: Quicker if rolling thin is not your thing
steps:
  - Pick the methi leaves off the stems, wash in two changes of water to get rid of grit, and chop fine. Do not wring them; a little moisture helps the dough.
  - In a bowl, combine the atta, besan, ground flaxseed and the dry masalas (turmeric, red chilli, coriander powder, ajwain, a pinch of hing, salt). Add the chopped methi and 2 tbsp of the curd and a few drops of the oil.
  - Mix with your fingers, adding water a spoon at a time, until you have a soft dough that is slightly sticky; methi releases water as it rests so start firmer than you think. Knead for 2 minutes, cover, and rest 15 minutes.
  - Divide the dough into four balls. Dust each with atta and roll thin, thinner than a roti, into a round the width of your outstretched hand. The dough makes four theplas, two per person.
  - Heat the tawa on medium-high. Cook a thepla for about 45 seconds until bubbles rise, flip, brush a few drops of oil over, flip again and press the edges with a spatula. Both sides should carry brown freckles and the thepla should stay soft, not crisp. About 2 minutes each.
  - Stack the cooked theplas in a cloth so they steam and stay pliable while you do the rest.
  - For the paneer, heat the tawa again with the last drops of oil. Lay the paneer slices down with a pinch of salt, turmeric and red chilli on top, and sear 1 to 2 minutes per side until golden. Do not overcook; low-fat paneer toughens quickly.
  - Whisk the remaining curd with a pinch of salt. Serve the theplas with the seared paneer and the curd on the side.
notes: Theplas stay soft for a day at room temperature, which is why they are the classic travel food; wrap in foil or cloth for tiffin. The paneer is best eaten fresh but is fine cold.
