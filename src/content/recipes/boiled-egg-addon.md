swaps:
  - for: Tawa-searing the halves
    use: Simply toss the peeled, halved eggs in the masala and lemon
    why: Zero-oil and faster, though the masala crust is nicer seared
  - for: Mixed dry masala
    use: Chaat masala with a pinch of black pepper
    why: Chaat masala already has the black salt and amchur that suit boiled eggs
steps:
  - Put both eggs in a small saucepan, cover them with cold water by an inch and set it on a high flame.
  - When the water comes to a rolling boil, lower the flame to a gentle simmer and cook for 9 minutes for a firm yolk, 7 if you like it slightly soft.
  - Move the eggs straight into a bowl of cold tap water and leave them for 5 minutes; this stops the grey ring on the yolk and makes peeling easy.
  - Crack the shells all over, peel under running water and halve the eggs lengthwise with a wet knife.
  - Heat a non-stick tawa on medium and rub a few drops of the oil across it. Lay the halves cut-side down and leave them for a minute until a golden crust forms.
  - Flip, sprinkle the masala over the cut sides, cook for another 30 seconds and take them off. Squeeze the lemon over just before eating.
  - "This is a non-veg add-on: cook it on its own tawa and serve it beside the vegetarian meal or as the snack it is planned for."
notes: Boiled eggs keep 3 days in the fridge in their shells; peel and season only when you eat. For a tiffin, pack the masala and lemon separately and season at the table.
