steps:
  - Soak the kala chana in three times its volume of water for 8 hours or overnight. Drain, put it in the pressure cooker with fresh water to cover by two fingers and a pinch of salt, and cook on high to the first whistle, then low for 6 to 7 whistles. Let the pressure drop on its own; the chana should be soft to the bite yet whole. Drain in a strainer.
  - Rinse the millet until the water runs clear and soak it for 10 minutes. Put it in a saucepan with 2.5 times its volume of water and a pinch of salt, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on the lowest flame for 12 to 15 minutes until the water is absorbed. Switch off and leave it covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
  - Dice the carrot small and crumble the paneer. Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin and keep it cold.
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add mustard seeds and let them crackle, then a pinch of urad dal, a broken dry red chilli, hing and a sprig of curry leaves. Stir for 20 seconds until the dal is light brown.
  - Add the carrot with a pinch of salt and a splash of water, cover and cook 3 minutes until just tender. Add the drained chana, turmeric and a little red chilli powder and toss on medium for 4 to 5 minutes until the chana is dry and coated with the tempering.
  - Fold in the crumbled paneer and warm it for a minute, then switch off and stir in the grated coconut and a squeeze of lemon if you have it.
  - Serve the sundal warm over or beside the millet, with a katori of curd for each person.
notes: Kala chana takes longer than kabuli; if it is still firm after 7 whistles, give it 2 more. Both the sundal and the millet keep 2 days in the fridge and pack well for a tiffin at room temperature; add the coconut fresh if packing.
