swaps:
  - for: Fresh palak
    use: Methi leaves or chopped cabbage
    why: Any leafy green wilts into moong dal the same way
  - for: Steaming vegetables in the idli stand
    use: Cook the lauki and carrot in the pressure cooker on top of the dal in a small steel katori
    why: One cooker, one wash
  - for: Lauki
    use: Zucchini or tinda
    why: Same texture, same steaming time
steps:
  - Rinse the moong dal three times and soak it for 15 minutes while you prepare the vegetables. Wash the palak thoroughly, discard thick stems and chop it roughly. Peel and cube the lauki and cut the carrot into thick batons. Cube the paneer.
  - Put the drained dal in the pressure cooker with turmeric, a pinch of hing, a slit green chilli and 2.5 times its volume of water. Cook on high to the first whistle, then low for 2 more whistles. Let the pressure drop on its own; moong should be soft and just breaking apart.
  - While the dal cooks, set the lauki and carrot in an idli stand or a strainer over a pot of boiling water, cover and steam 8 to 10 minutes until a knife slides in easily. Sprinkle them with salt, pepper and a pinch of chaat masala while hot.
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add cumin seeds and wait until they darken and smell nutty, then add grated ginger and a pinch of red chilli powder. Add the chopped palak straight away with a pinch of salt and stir on high for 2 to 3 minutes until it wilts and turns a deep green.
  - Pour in the cooked dal, add salt and coriander powder, and simmer on low for 5 minutes, thinning with hot water to a pourable consistency. Whisk the dal briskly with the ladle so the palak breaks up into it.
  - Add the paneer cubes and let them warm through for 2 minutes. Squeeze in a little lemon if you have it, sprinkle garam masala and switch off.
  - Rest the dal for 5 minutes with the lid on. Serve in bowls with the steamed vegetables on the side and a small katori of curd for each person.
notes: Palak turns dull if cooked too long after the dal goes in, so keep the final simmer short. The dal thickens overnight; loosen with water to reheat. Steamed vegetables are best fresh, but the dal packs well for a tiffin.
