swaps:
  - for: Firm tofu
    use: Low-fat paneer, same weight
    why: Easier to find; skip the pressing
  - for: Fresh palak
    use: Frozen chopped spinach, thawed and squeezed, or 2 katori chopped methi
    why: Frozen saves the washing and blanching
  - for: Hand-patted jowar roti
    use: Whole wheat roti, rolled
    why: Jowar dough is fragile for beginners
steps:
  - Press the tofu in a muslin cloth under a plate for 10 minutes, then cut into 1-inch cubes and toss with a pinch of salt and turmeric. Pick over the palak, discard thick stems and wash in three changes of water.
  - Bring a saucepan of water to a boil, drop in the palak for 90 seconds until it turns bright green and limp, then lift it straight into a bowl of cold water. Squeeze out the excess, and blend in the mixie with a green chilli or a pinch of chilli powder and a spoon of water to a coarse puree. Keep it coarse; a fine puree tastes grassy.
  - "Make the jowar dough: put the flour in a bowl with a pinch of salt, add hot water a spoon at a time and knead 3 to 4 minutes until smooth and pliable. Cover with a damp cloth."
  - Heat a non-stick kadhai on high with a third of the oil. Add the tofu cubes in one layer and leave 2 minutes before turning; brown each side until golden, then lift out.
  - Lower to medium, add the remaining oil and cumin seeds; when they crackle, add the onion and cook 4 minutes until soft. Add the ginger-garlic paste and stir 30 seconds until the raw smell goes, then the tomato and the spice powders (turmeric, coriander, a pinch of garam masala). Cook 4 minutes until the tomato melts.
  - Pour in the palak puree, add salt, and simmer on low 5 minutes, uncovered, stirring so it does not spit. The colour should stay green; overcooking turns it olive. Slide in the tofu, simmer 3 minutes more so it soaks up the gravy, and switch off. A spoon of curd or a squeeze of lemon at the end lifts it.
  - For the rotis, divide the dough into 2 balls. Pat each on a sheet of plastic or the board with wet palms into a round the thickness of a coin. Cook on a hot tawa 1 minute, flip when spotted, 1 minute more, then hold over the flame with tongs a few seconds to puff.
  - Serve each person one jowar roti with a generous share of the palak tofu.
notes: Blanch and shock the palak quickly and keep the simmer short, or the dish loses its colour. The palak gravy keeps a day chilled and can be made ahead; add fresh-browned tofu when reheating. Jowar rotis go hard as they cool, so cook them last.
