swaps:
  - for: Fresh grated coconut
    use: Desiccated coconut, 1.5 tbsp, soaked in 2 tbsp warm water for 10 minutes
    why: Keeps in the cupboard when fresh coconut is a hassle
  - for: Shredding cabbage by hand
    use: Pulse chunks in the mixie for 2 short bursts
    why: Cuts prep to a minute; stop before it turns to mush
  - for: Buttermilk (chaas)
    use: Half a katori of plain curd whisked with a glass of water
    why: Chaas is just thinned curd; make it fresh in a minute
steps:
  - Shred the cabbage finely and grate the carrot. Crumble the paneer with your fingers into small bits. Grind or hand-crush the grated coconut with a green chilli, a pinch of cumin and a pinch of turmeric from the masala mix into a coarse, damp mixture.
  - For the chaas, whisk the buttermilk with a pinch of roasted cumin powder, black salt and a few curry leaves or coriander if you like, and put it in the fridge to chill.
  - Heat the oil in a non-stick kadhai on medium. Add mustard seeds first and wait for them to pop, then a pinch of urad dal if you keep it, a broken dry red chilli and a sprig of curry leaves. Stir for 20 seconds until the dal turns light brown.
  - Add the cabbage and carrot with salt, toss to coat, sprinkle two spoons of water over the top and cover. Cook on low for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring once, until the cabbage is tender but still has a slight bite and a bright colour.
  - Uncover, raise the heat a little and add the coconut mixture. Toss for 2 minutes until the coconut is warmed through and any water has dried off; the thoran should look loose and dry, not wet.
  - Fold in the crumbled paneer, cook for another minute just to heat it and switch off. Rest covered for 3 minutes.
  - Serve the thoran warm with a glass of the chilled chaas per person.
notes: Do not overcook the cabbage; it should smell sweet, not sulphurous. The thoran packs well for a tiffin and is fine at room temperature, but keep the chaas in a bottle in the fridge and drink it fresh.
