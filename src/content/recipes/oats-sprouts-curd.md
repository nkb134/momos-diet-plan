steps:
  - Dry-roast the oats in the kadhai on medium for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until they smell nutty and feel dry and crisp between the fingers. Tip them into a bowl. Roasting keeps the upma from turning gummy.
  - Rinse the sprouts and steam them 3 to 4 minutes in a steamer or in a strainer set over a pan of boiling water, until the raw smell goes but they still have a slight bite. Set aside. Dice the carrot and beans finely.
  - Heat all the oil in the same kadhai on medium. Add mustard seeds first and wait until they finish popping, then the peanuts; toast 1 minute until they darken a shade. Follow with a pinch of urad dal if you keep it, curry leaves, a slit green chilli or a pinch of chilli from the mix, and hing.
  - Add the carrot and beans with a splash of water, cover and cook 4 minutes until the carrot is just tender. Add turmeric and salt.
  - Pour in about two katori of water, bring to a rolling boil, then lower the heat and rain in the roasted oats while stirring so no lumps form. Stir 2 to 3 minutes until the oats swell and the mixture pulls away from the sides.
  - Fold in the steamed sprouts, cover, and leave on the lowest flame for 2 minutes. Switch off and rest 3 minutes; the upma firms up as it sits.
  - Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt and serve it in a katori alongside the warm upma, with a squeeze of lemon over the top.
notes: Oats upma tightens as it cools; if packing for tiffin, keep it slightly looser than you like and carry the curd separately. Sprouts and roasted oats can both be prepared the night before.
