steps:
  - "The evening before: rinse the millet and the urad dal separately and soak each in plenty of water for 5 to 6 hours, adding a pinch of methi seeds to the urad."
  - Grind the urad first in the mixie with a little cold water in short bursts until it is fluffy and smooth, then grind the millet to a slightly coarse paste. Mix both in a large bowl with salt, beat with your hand for a minute to work in air, and leave it covered in a warm spot for 8 to 12 hours until it has risen and smells pleasantly sour.
  - "In the morning, start the sambar. Rinse the toor dal and pressure cook it with turmeric and 3 times its volume of water: high to the first whistle, then low for 3 whistles. Let the pressure drop and whisk the dal smooth."
  - While the dal cooks, soak the tamarind in half a glass of warm water, squeeze and strain it. Cut the drumstick into finger lengths and dice the carrot.
  - In a kadhai, simmer the drumstick and carrot in the tamarind water with a glass of plain water, salt, a pinch of turmeric and the sambar powder from the masala mix for 10 minutes until the drumstick flesh is soft enough to scrape off with a spoon.
  - Pour in the mashed dal, adjust to a pouring consistency with hot water, and simmer 5 minutes more until the raw tamarind sharpness mellows. For the tempering, heat the oil in a small pan on medium, add mustard seeds and let them pop, then a pinch of methi seeds, a dry red chilli, hing and curry leaves for 10 seconds, and pour it over the sambar.
  - Stir the batter gently once and ladle it into the greased idli stand, filling each mould three-quarters. Steam in the pressure cooker without the whistle, or a steamer, over boiling water on medium for 12 minutes; a toothpick should come out clean. Rest 3 minutes before scooping the idlis out with a wet spoon. The batter makes 8 to 10 idlis.
  - Whisk the curd with a pinch of salt. Serve 4 or 5 idlis per person with a bowl of hot sambar and a katori of curd.
notes: Sambar thickens as it stands, so loosen it with hot water before serving. Leftover batter keeps 3 days in the fridge, and idlis with sambar make a reliable tiffin; pack the sambar in a leak-proof box and the curd separately.
